01 · The claim

Price the clause.

The same $1,000 contract costs $1,187.37 or $1,256.22 a month six years later. The only difference is one paragraph.

That paragraph says how the price moves each year: with the mechanic's wage, by a fixed percent, or with consumer prices. This page measures what each choice was worth, on public data from 2019 to 2025.

02 · The formula

The formula follows a public wage rate.

The formula, in plain words

New price = labor part × rate plus benefits now ÷ at signing + material part × metals index now ÷ at signing

Labor is 93.29% of the starting price and material 6.71%. A $1,000 contract signed in January 2019, at its first adjustment in January 2020: $932.90 × 93.235 ÷ 90.315 + $67.10 × 216.9 ÷ 227.2 = $1,027.12.

06 · What to run on

Three numbers for the book, one rule for the renewal.

Cost recovery
The price change since signing, divided by the formula's change since signing. Under 100% the contract is losing ground. A fixed 3% signed in January 2019 stands at 98.08%.
Escalation capture
The increase billed, divided by the increase the clause allowed. It shows the increases that were waived, asked short or sent late. Under an index limit it was worth 2.46 points.
Dollars to recover at renewal
The gap to the formula, times the monthly price, sorted by renewal date. That is the work list, and the red blocks above are the top of it.

How it was counted
Each January the labor part of the price moves with the rate and benefits dated that January 1, and the index parts with the December before.
The labor and material shares are the ones written in the agreement: 93.29% and 6.71% of the price.
A day-one discount is the cut in the starting price that leaves the provider with the same total, from signing through December 2024.
The page computes every figure from the series on the left, and each one was counted a second time with separate code.
