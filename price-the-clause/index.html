Pricing noteElevator maintenance contracts · public data, 2019 to 2025

Capping price rises at inflation sounds fair. Six years on, the price sits below both cost and inflation.

A CPI cap lets a maintenance price rise each January by the contract's own cost formula or by inflation, whichever is lower. On public data, a contract signed in 2019 ended 2.47% below its cost formula. The price falls behind in the years inflation dips, and nothing in the clause wins it back.

When prices run ahead, the provider gets cost and no more.

In January 2022 and 2023 consumer prices jumped 7.04% and 6.45%. The clause paid the formula's 5.77% and 2.20% instead.

Step 3 of 4

When prices fall behind, the provider eats the difference.

January 2021: the formula said 3.48%, consumer prices said 1.36%. The clause paid 1.36%.

Step 4 of 4

Small losses, never won back.

Three Januaries came in under the formula: 2020 by 0.42%, 2021 by 2.05% and 2025 by 0.01%. Nothing in the clause gives them back. Compounded, the price ends 2.47% under the formula.

02 · Why it leaks

Pay moves like a clock. Prices do not.

The mechanic's pay rose by almost the same amount every January, through a pandemic and an inflation spike. Consumer prices swung more than fivefold. A clause that pays the lower of a steady number and a swinging one loses each time the swing dips, and gains nothing when it climbs.

Pay is the union elevator mechanic's hourly rate plus benefits, as the U.S. Department of Labor publishes it each January; metals is the government's metals price index. A $1,000 contract signed in January 2019 moved to $1,027.12 at its first adjustment.

06 · Price the clause

A clause is a price. Quote it like one.

Each clause, turned into the starting price change that would have left the provider level with the formula over six years. One contract, signed in January 2019, paid through December 2024.

When a buyer asks for a CPI cap, the ask has a size: 1.79% on the starting price, on these six years. Put it in the quote, or trade it for something worth as much.

Which way a CPI cap cuts is certain, because the lower of two numbers is never above either. How far it cuts depends on how much inflation swings.

07 · Every renewal

One rule for every renewal.

The renewal rule

Greenat most 0.50% behind the formulaLeave the price. Keep the clause.
Yellow0.50% to 2.00% behindClose the gap at renewal, in one step or two.
Redmore than 2.00% behindRe-price to the formula, and replace the clause or charge for it.

It needs two fields any contract system already holds: the date signed or last reset, and the clause type. How the increase is asked for matters too. Under a CPI cap, asking each year for the whole gap back brought the price level with the formula by 2023; asking only for that year's rise left it 2.47% behind.
