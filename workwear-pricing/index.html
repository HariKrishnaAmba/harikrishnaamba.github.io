05 · Next

What I would do with a pricing team's own data

  1. Swap the public indexes for actual cost by garment family and fabric. Flame-resistant cotton blends, aramids and modacrylics are different cost chains. The clause weights should follow the mix each account buys.
  2. Build the contract calendar. Every account with its term, renewal date, clause and cap, so margin at risk reads by account and by quarter instead of in total.
  3. Put a price on the term in every bid. Fixed, capped and indexed versions of the same quote, each with the day-one price that makes them equal, so the customer chooses with the number in front of them.
  4. Watch the mills, not the invoice. A move in fabric is about six months of notice. A monthly alert on the index turns that notice into a repricing list.

06 · Method

How I measured it

  • Data. Six public monthly series, retrieved from FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics: producer price index for men's and boys' work clothing made from purchased fabrics; producer price index for broadwoven fabric mills; producer price index for artificial fibers and filaments; import price index for apparel manufacturing; consumer price index, all items, all urban consumers. International Monetary Fund: global price of cotton.
  • Pass-through. The monthly change in the downstream price is regressed on the change in the upstream price of the same month and the twelve months before, in logs (24 months for cotton to work clothing). The fabric to work clothing link is also run on quarterly averages with four lags. Standard errors allow for autocorrelation (Newey-West).
  • A program. The price is set at the cost of the signing month. The program runs the 36 months after it, with the same quantity each month. Cost not recovered is the sum over those months of cost above price, per $1 million of yearly cost. Bought-in garments follow the work clothing index; garments made in house follow the fabric index.
  • A clause. At each anniversary the price moves by the change in the named index since the last adjustment. A half-yearly clause does the same every six months. A cap limits each yearly rise; the price still follows the index down.
  • Signing months.
  • Runs in fabric. A run is an eight-month rise in the fabric index at least as large as the one to August 2026. The count of earlier runs uses the first month each one reached that size.
