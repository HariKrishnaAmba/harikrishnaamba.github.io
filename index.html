Case 1 of 3 · Price guidance

Sales wasn't discounting. The price guidance was wrong.

4business units analyzed
321,633rows validated, 0 exceptions
8,346customers segmented
The belief
A large share of revenue was billed below the price floor, and leadership read it as reps giving away price.
What I did
Built the Price Guidance Performance analysis in Power BI on SAP data, placing every invoiced price against its floor, target and stretch, then tested whether the gap came from deals or from the guidance itself.
What I found
Most of the below-floor revenue came from guidance set out of line with where the market actually transacts, not from discounting.
Why it mattered
The conversation moved from policing the sales team to fixing the price structure. Product management leadership validated it, and it became a standing quarterly band review.

Case 2 of 3 · Price correction

Found the customer prices leaking margin, then fixed them in the system.

81,375corrected prices loaded
99.85%loaded successfully
1 weekfrom method to accepted review
The problem
Some customer-specific registered prices sat below the company's price guidance, so margin leaked on every order that used them.
What I did
Found the true outliers with a statistical test on registered prices, designed a method that sorted them by revenue and gave each group a clear action, and got that method approved.
What changed
Built a review file for each regional sales leader, then carried the corrections through to the load into the pricing system.
Why it mattered
A diagnosis became an actual price change. Pricing and analytics leadership accepted the review within a week, and the corrected prices are now what customers are billed.

Case 3 of 3 · Price increase realization

A manual export became the weekly file of record for the executive team.

1.6Mrows migrated
700,570lines reconciled in one week
20,257,051cells, 0 mismatches
The belief
The weekly price increase report came from a manual dashboard export that nobody could trace back to the source system.
What I did
Reconstructed the undocumented logic behind a 122-column query and a 35-step transformation, rebuilt it from SAP HANA in SQL and Python, and reconciled it line by line.
What I found
The manual pull had been quietly leaving in-scope revenue out. The rebuilt pipeline matched the legacy output to the penny, then found what it had missed.
Why it mattered
A 27-step automated pipeline now runs every week in about 19 minutes and shows leadership exactly which pricing conditions hold an increase back.
