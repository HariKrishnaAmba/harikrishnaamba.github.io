What a pricing team can do with itSet the response rule by market, not nationally. In Chicago the street has caught up within three weeks, so the room to lead or to hold is short. 05 · Next

What I would do with a pricing team's own data

  1. Swap the public marker for rack and delivered cost by terminal. Then the gap splits into basis, rack margin and street margin, and the 2026 shift can be placed on the right link of the chain.
  2. Split it by channel. Where sites are company operated, the supplier carries the lag. Where a marketer or dealer sets the street price, they carry it. Rack data shows where that line falls in each market.
  3. Run it daily, by site and competitor set. That catches the Tuesday restoration and shows where a brand leads price moves and where it follows them.
  4. Add volume. Holding price after a wholesale fall earns margin and costs gallons. With volume the trade can be priced instead of argued.

06 · Method

How I measured it

  • Data. US Energy Information Administration, public files. Weekly retail price of regular gasoline for the ten cities EIA surveys (Monday survey) and daily wholesale spot prices, which EIA publishes from Refinitiv, an LSEG business.
  • Benchmarks. Each city is set against the nearest wholesale marker EIA publishes: New York Harbor conventional regular for Boston and New York; Gulf Coast conventional regular for Chicago, Cleveland, Denver, Houston and Miami; Los Angeles RBOB for Los Angeles, San Francisco and Seattle. The ranking is repeated against Gulf Coast gasoline and against Brent crude for all ten.
  • Timing. Each Monday pump price is matched to the average wholesale price of the trading week that ended the Friday before.
  • Model. The weekly change in the pump price is regressed on the wholesale change of the same week and of the eight weeks before, in cents per gallon. Rises and falls get separate coefficients in the direction test. Standard errors allow for autocorrelation (Newey-West, eight lags).
  • Street margin in play. For each of the nine surveys after a 10¢ move, the part of the move not yet in the pump price, added up and multiplied by one million gallons a week. It is what the street side gives up after a rise or keeps after a fall, against passing the move on at once. Responses are held so they never step back, and capped at 100%.
  • Taxes. Fixed per-gallon taxes do not affect the speed results. Illinois also applies its sales tax to gasoline (6.25% at the state rate, with local sales taxes on top), and a percentage tax turns a 10¢ move before tax into a little more than 10¢ at the pump. That is why Chicago ends slightly above 100%.
  • What the gap holds. The gap between pump and wholesale holds everything after the spot market: basis to the local market, rack and retail margin, freight, taxes and, in Washington since 2023, the cost of carbon allowances. I track changes in it.
  • Sources beyond the price files. 2026 market events: EIA, Today in Energy and Short-Term Energy Outlook. Washington allowance prices: Department of Ecology auction results, June and September 2026. Pacific Northwest supply: EIA, PADD 5 Transportation Fuels Markets. Price cycles: Lewis and Noel, “The Speed of Gasoline Price Response in Markets with and without Edgeworth Cycles”, Review of Economics and Statistics, 2011.
